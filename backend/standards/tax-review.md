# Tax Responsibility and Non-Reimbursable Tax Review

Determine whether each tax, sales-tax charge, use-tax charge, excise tax, local tax, or similar assessment is:

* Included within the contractor's original contract price.
* Reimbursable by the owner.
* Exempt due to the owner's tax-exempt status.
* Payable by the contractor without additional compensation.
* Payable by a subcontractor or supplier.
* Included within subcontractor or supplier pricing.
* Separately permitted as a pass-through cost.
* Eligible for markup.
* Prohibited from markup.
* Unsupported or incorrectly billed.

Do not assume that a tax appearing on a subcontractor or supplier invoice is automatically reimbursable by the owner.

## Contract Tax Allocation

Extract and cite all relevant contract language concerning: sales tax; use tax; tax exemptions; tax-exemption certificates; contractor responsibility for taxes; taxes included in the contract sum; taxes excluded from the contract sum; changes in tax rates; taxes on materials; taxes on equipment rental; taxes on labor; taxes on freight or delivery; taxes related to change orders; taxes on contractor markup; taxes on subcontractor markup; tax documentation requirements.

Classify each tax charge as: owner reimbursable; contractor responsibility; subcontractor responsibility; supplier responsibility; included in lump-sum price; exempt; pending contractual interpretation; unsupported; unable to determine.

## Contractor-Absorbed Tax Test

When the contract states or implies that the contractor is responsible for applicable taxes, or that the contract amount includes all taxes, calculate:

Approved Contract or Change-Order Price − Contractually Included Tax = Contractor's Remaining Net Revenue

The contractor must absorb the included tax within the approved price and must not add the tax again as a separate owner charge.

Flag any instance where:

* Tax is separately billed even though the contract price is tax-inclusive.
* The contractor adds tax above an approved lump-sum amount.
* A subcontractor invoice includes tax and the GC passes it through even though the GC contract requires the contractor to absorb it.
* Tax is included in direct cost and then added again separately.
* Tax is included in a subcontractor proposal and added again by the GC.
* Tax is charged on a tax-exempt purchase without explanation.
* The contractor failed to use an available owner tax-exemption certificate.
* Tax is marked up when the contract prohibits markup on taxes.
* Tax is calculated on overhead, profit, insurance, bond, or another non-taxable component without contractual or statutory support.
* The tax rate does not match the invoiced taxable base.
* The contractor applies tax to both direct cost and the total marked-up amount.
* A change-order proposal adds tax that was already included in subcontractor quotations.
* Credit change orders fail to return previously billed tax.
* Tax is shifted into another line item to avoid showing it separately.

## Tax Reconciliation

For every tax-bearing invoice or change-order proposal, show:

Taxable Direct Cost × Applicable Tax Rate = Expected Tax

Then compare: supplier- or subcontractor-reported tax; GC-reported tax; contractually reimbursable tax; tax included within the approved contract amount; tax that must be absorbed by the contractor; excess or unsupported tax.

## Tax-Inclusive Change Orders

When a change order is approved as a lump-sum, not-to-exceed, or fully burdened amount, determine whether the approved amount already includes: taxes; overhead; profit; insurance; bond; freight; escalation; other fees.

Do not permit the contractor to add tax above the approved change-order amount unless the approved document expressly allows it.

Example:

Approved Tax-Inclusive Change Order: $100,000
Subcontractor Direct Cost: $90,000
Included Tax and Permitted Markup: $10,000

Maximum Owner Obligation: $100,000

The contractor may not request $100,000 plus additional tax unless the contract or executed change order expressly authorizes it.

## Tax-Exempt Owner Review

When the owner may be tax-exempt:

* Verify whether an exemption certificate was provided.
* Identify which purchases qualify for exemption.
* Determine whether the contractor or subcontractor was required to use the certificate.
* Identify taxes caused by the contractor's failure to properly use available exemption documentation.
* Treat avoidable taxes as contractor responsibility unless the contract states otherwise.
* Flag taxes paid without evidence that the purchase was taxable.
* Flag tax exemption savings not credited to the owner when required.

Do not make a final tax-law determination without confirming the applicable jurisdiction and authoritative tax requirements. When tax treatment cannot be conclusively verified, classify it as requiring tax or legal review rather than approving the charge.

## Required Tax Finding

For every material tax charge, explicitly state one of the following:

* Properly reimbursable by the owner.
* Included in the approved contract amount and must be absorbed by the contractor.
* Included in the subcontractor's price and cannot be billed again.
* Exempt and should be removed.
* Incorrectly calculated.
* Unsupported.
* Requires additional documentation.
* Requires jurisdiction-specific tax review.

Include all contractor-absorbed taxes as a separate line in the recommended payment adjustment:

Contractor Requested Current Payment − Tax Included in Contract but Separately Rebilled − Duplicate Tax − Exempt or Avoidable Tax − Excess Tax or Tax Markup = Tax-Adjusted Payment Amount
